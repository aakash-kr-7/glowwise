Practical buying guidance

Use comfort and tolerance as the first filter

A fragrant lotion may be enjoyable, but scent is optional. If fragrance is something you avoid, start with the verified fragrance-free record rather than relying on a food-inspired name or a “natural” story. Niacinamide, shea butter, glycerin and named oils are formulation facts where verified; they do not let us promise relief from a diagnosed skin condition.

Make application part of a simple routine

AAD dry-skin guidance recommends applying moisturiser after washing while skin remains damp and choosing gentle care. Persistent cracking, bleeding or discomfort is a reason to seek qualified advice. We do not prescribe treatment from a product card. A lotion’s marketing around firmness or glow is separate from the ordinary task of moisturising.

Compare scented choices honestly

Caramello, Vanilla Vibes and Hazelnut are not the same scent merely because all sound gourmand. Hawaiian Rumba has a fruity floral direction; Tropic is a separate gel-cream positioning. The manufacturer’s scent descriptions help you shortlist, but no online note list can tell you whether you will enjoy a fragrance on your skin. Prefer a manageable first purchase over a multi-bottle bundle.
