Start with wash day, not a miracle claim

A shampoo has a specific job: cleaning your scalp and hair. If your lengths feel dry or frizzy, the useful question is which cleansing option fits the rest of your routine—not which bottle promises to erase every flyaway. A conditioning step, handling and your own hair texture remain part of that decision.

The American Academy of Dermatology’s healthy-hair guidance distinguishes cleaning the scalp from conditioning the lengths and advises a routine appropriate to hair type. We use that distinction as a starting point, without prescribing a universal washing frequency.

Compare the quantity as well as the price

A ₹198 bottle and a ₹276 bottle do not necessarily represent the same amount of shampoo. In this check, the first is 100 ml and the second is 250 ml. Divide the item price by volume only when both quantities are expressed in the same unit. Delivery can also change the actual amount you pay.

A smaller bottle can be sensible when trying a new format. A larger bottle may lower cost per volume once you already know you like it. Neither calculation proves that one formula is better. What to look for in your own routine

  1. Separate scalp needs from the feel of your lengths. An oily scalp and dry ends need not be treated as contradictory observations.
  2. Read the current full label, especially if you already know an ingredient or fragrance does not suit you. We have not verified fragrance-free status for these four records.
  3. Change one purchase decision at a time so you can understand your preference. Keep notes about the pack and routine you used rather than expecting an immediate universal result.
  4. Consider whether you are comparing conditioning positioning with an active scalp shampoo. They are different reasons to buy, even though both are shampoos.

What this shortlist leaves out

Our catalog also contains an active anti-dandruff shampoo, but an active scalp concern is not the same decision as an ordinary dry-length routine. We do not diagnose flaking, persistent itching or hair loss. Seek qualified advice for continuing scalp problems rather than interpreting a shopping guide as treatment.
