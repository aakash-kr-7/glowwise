Make the decision simpler, not more confident than the evidence

“Sensitive skin” is a reason to be careful about assumptions. A cleanser’s gentle or fragrance-free label can help you investigate a product, but it cannot guarantee your personal tolerance. A shopping guide cannot diagnose why your skin feels uncomfortable.

The American Academy of Dermatology’s face-washing guidance recommends gentle cleansing with lukewarm water and fingertips, without scrubbing. We use that basic distinction to separate ordinary cleansing from an unnecessary search for the strongest active formula.

Why an active cleanser is not the default here

The same catalog includes a salicylic-acid/LHA cleanser and a rice-water/niacinamide face wash. Their presence in the cleanser category does not make them automatic sensitive-skin recommendations. An ingredient name, a brightening claim or a low item price is not evidence of suitability for already irritated skin.

Our verified-attribute filter only uses explicit recorded facts. An unknown fragrance-free status does not pass the fragrance-free filter. It is not interpreted as proof that the product contains fragrance, either; unknown is a distinct state.

Read the label in context

  1. Check the current formulation and your known sensitivities. A fragrance-free statement is one attribute, not a complete personal safety assessment.
  2. Do not infer that low foaming means inadequate cleansing, or that stronger foaming means a better result. We have not tested removal performance for these records.
  3. Avoid changing several product decisions at once if it makes your own preference harder to understand. Keep track of the exact product and pack you considered.
  4. If irritation continues or you have a diagnosed condition, obtain qualified advice rather than relying on a category label.
