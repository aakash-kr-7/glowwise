The decision this guide helps you make

Sensitive-feeling skin is not a diagnosis, and a cleanser name cannot predict your tolerance. This guide keeps the four approved cleanser records and separates two verified fragrance-free starting points from two conditional alternatives. We have not added questionable recommendations just to make the count six.

How we selected and ordered the recommendations

Minimalist B12 + Oat leads for its fragrance-free, low-foam positioning, followed by Aquaporin for a fragrance-free foaming preference. Plum Rice Water remains an alternative with fragrance status not verified here. Salicylic + LHA appears last to explain why an active exfoliating cleanser is a different decision, rather than a default sensitive-skin recommendation. This is a cautious suitability sequence, not a clinical safety ranking.

1. Minimalist B12 + Oat Extract 6.5% Cleanser — First investigation for low-foam cleansing

Minimalist's B12 + Oat Extract 6.5% Gentle Cleanser is a creamy gel with a low-lather description, fragrance-free label and published pH range of 5.5–6.5. Its oat-based cleansing description is more directly relevant to a simple sensitive-skin routine than an exfoliating-acid promise.

At the same checked item price as Aquaporin, this gives 120 ml rather than 100 ml, although volume alone cannot establish how long either bottle lasts. Choose it for a low-foam preference and inspect the complete ingredient list for your known sensitivities. The legacy source URL says oat-extract-06, while the current page names 6.5%; Glowwise follows the current label without creating a duplicate record.

Fragrance-free and gentle labels cannot guarantee tolerance or establish treatment for an impaired skin barrier.

2. Minimalist Aquaporin Booster 5% Cleanser — For a mild foaming preference

Aquaporin Booster 5% is the alternative for someone who prefers a mild foaming cleanser. Minimalist lists a pH of 5.5, fragrance-free status and a hydration-focused formula with glyceryl glucoside and glycerin. The word aquaporin describes the manufacturer's formulation story; Glowwise has not measured moisture transport in skin.

The 100 ml bottle costs the same as the oat cleanser's 120 ml bottle at this check, so the reason to prefer it is the cleansing format rather than pack value. Use your existing experience with foam, rinsing and post-wash tightness as a practical decision aid. Keep the rest of your routine stable when evaluating a new cleanser so you are not changing several products at once.

A foaming format is a preference, not evidence of deeper cleaning or better tolerance. No comparative sensitive-skin trial was reviewed.

3. Plum 2% Niacinamide & Rice Water Face Wash — Conditional niacinamide cleanser alternative

Plum's 2% niacinamide and rice-water face wash uses a sulphate-free label and is sold in identified 100 ml and 150 ml packs. Its published ingredient information also names cleansing agents and humectants. That gives a real formulation to investigate, rather than relying only on brightening language.

We keep it as a comparison alternative, below the two explicitly fragrance-free Minimalist choices for this particular guide. Fragrance-free status was not established by our check, so it does not pass that filter. The product is rinsed off: a named niacinamide concentration is not proof that the cleanser will reproduce results from a leave-on product. Review the current full label rather than assuming every rice-water formula is identical.

The brand's user-study and brightening claims do not establish individual tolerance or a leave-on treatment result.

4. Minimalist Salicylic Acid + LHA 2% Cleanser — Conditional option for a separate exfoliation need

This fragrance-free cleanser combines salicylic acid and LHA under a 2% product label, with a published pH range of 4.5–5.5. It belongs in the comparison to show why a familiar brand and a low price do not make every cleanser the same kind of choice.

It is not our default recommendation for a reader whose main concern is sensitivity or stinging. Consider an exfoliating cleanser only for a distinct reason, taking account of products you already use and advice for any diagnosed condition. The 100 ml and 250 ml variants have different upfront costs. Do not treat the combined 2% label as a verified 2% concentration for each individual acid.

An exfoliating formula adds an active ingredient decision. It is not interchangeable with the low-foam oat cleanser.

Practical buying guidance

A gentler routine starts with how you wash

AAD guidance recommends a gentle, non-abrasive cleanser, lukewarm water and fingertips rather than scrubbing. More foam, hotter water or a rough cloth is not evidence of a better clean. If your face feels tight after washing, review both your technique and the product instead of adding another exfoliant immediately.

Know what the verified labels do and do not mean

B12 + Oat’s stated pH is 5.5–6.5; Aquaporin’s is 5.5. These are manufacturer specifications, not a guarantee against irritation. Fragrance-free status helps identify an excluded ingredient choice but does not establish that every other ingredient will suit you. Read the full current label, particularly if you already know a personal trigger.

Do not quietly replace a simple cleanser with an active

Salicylic + LHA has a stated pH of 4.5–5.5 and an active-cleansing position. That does not make it superior for a sensitive-skin brief. If you are using other actives or have a continuing reaction, qualified advice is more useful than combining several shopping recommendations. This guide does not diagnose redness, prescribe treatment or label an unfamiliar formula safe for everyone.

Common questions

Why are there four products rather than six?

The approved catalog already has these four cleanser records. The six core categories have six products each; this seventh guide is an additional topic. We retain its useful scope without inventing two new sensitive-skin recommendations.

Which should I investigate first?

B12 + Oat for the low-foam preference or Aquaporin for the mild-foam preference. Neither is a promise of personal tolerance.

Is Plum Rice Water fragrance-free?

That status remains unknown in this research. Do not select it solely on an assumption that a rice-water name implies a fragrance-free formula.
